# The medium

Letterpress on heavy cotton rag paper, photographed on a wooden desk by a window, with one desk lamp. Every state of the piece is held to these rules.

## Edges

- **The sheet** is guillotine cut: straight, with a visible thickness (0.9 mm) and a hard contact line where it meets the desk. Its shadow is never uniform, because the paper is never flat: corners stand a millimetre or three off the desk and one long edge rises in a slow wave.
- **A letter** has soft shoulders. The forme pressed it into the sheet, so its edge is a small slope, lit on the side facing away from the light and shadowed on the side facing it. Heavier type bites deeper than text type.
- **Under the loupe** letter edges stay crisp; the ink does not blur into the paper.

## Colour

- Two inks, mixed by hand: a warm black and an oxblood red. No pure black, no pure white.
- Ink is pressed, not painted. It is densest at the edge of a stroke. In the middle of a large solid it is thinner, follows the tooth of the paper, and misses the odd speck.
- The second colour is a separate pass through the press and sits a hair out of register.
- Paper is a warm white that takes its colour from the light: cream in sun, cool in window shade, amber under the lamp.

## Surface

- Cotton fibre, visible as relief in raking light and as faint mottling in flat light.
- Ink is slightly smoother than paper, never glossy.
- The desk is oiled, worn wood with its own grain and scars. Brass is turned, with lathe marks.

## Flaws

- Slight misregistration of the red.
- Uneven ink in solids.
- The paper's own curl.

Sparingly. This is fine work, not distressed.

## Motion

- Paper is stiff. It bends about one axis at a time, as a cylinder rolled along a straight crease, and never stretches. (`sheet/curl.ts` has a test that proves it.)
- It slides with friction and swings behind the point it is dragged by. Released, it coasts and stops.
- A lifted corner falls back if let go early. Past the tipping point the sheet carries itself over, then drops flat.
- Objects lift a little when picked up and are set down, not dropped.

## Light

- Day: the sun through a window (with its glazing bars), the cool glow of the window itself, and the sky. Night: the lamp, and a trace of moonlight.
- Nothing glows. No bloom. Highlights are small and physical.
- The lamp sits low so that it always rakes the impression.

## Nothing else

Nothing is on screen that is not part of the photograph, apart from one fading hint line and a row of small controls.
